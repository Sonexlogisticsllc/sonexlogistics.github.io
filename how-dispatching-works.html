What does a freight dispatcher do?

A freight dispatcher keeps a truck loaded and paid. This is how dispatching works from the first load search to the day you get paid.

Who is who in a load

  • Shipper: the business that has freight to move.
  • Broker: a licensed intermediary that connects shippers with carriers.
  • Carrier: the trucking company or owner-operator that hauls the freight.
  • Dispatcher: represents the carrier, finding and negotiating loads and managing the details.

A dispatch service like Sonex is neither a broker nor a carrier. It works on behalf of the carrier.

The life of a load, step by step

  • 1. Find. The dispatcher searches load boards and broker relationships for freight that fits your equipment, lanes and home time.
  • 2. Screen. Each load is checked for rate per mile, deadhead, pickup and delivery windows and the broker's payment history.
  • 3. Negotiate. The dispatcher negotiates the rate and extras such as detention, layover and tarp fees.
  • 4. You approve. You see the load details and decide. With Sonex, nothing is booked without your approval.
  • 5. Rate confirmation. The broker sends a rate confirmation, the document that states the agreed price and terms. Read it before pickup.
  • 6. Pickup and transit. You load, sign the bill of lading (BOL) and drive. The dispatcher handles check calls and updates.
  • 7. Delivery. You deliver and obtain a signed proof of delivery (POD).
  • 8. Invoice and payment. The invoice goes out with the paperwork attached, and the dispatcher follows up until you are paid.

Terms you will hear

  • Deadhead: miles driven empty.
  • Detention: payment for time spent waiting beyond the free time at a dock.
  • Layover: payment when you must wait overnight or longer for a load.
  • TONU (truck ordered, not used): compensation when a load is cancelled after you were dispatched.
  • Lumper: a third-party loading or unloading fee.
  • Quick pay: a faster payment option, usually for a fee.

Where a good dispatcher earns their fee

The value is not in finding any load, it is in finding the right load and removing everything else from your plate: the rate negotiation, the broker calls, the rate confirmations, the invoices. Time you are not spending on the phone is time you spend driving or resting.
