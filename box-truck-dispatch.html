Why box trucks are in demand

Straight trucks fit places a tractor-trailer cannot: tight docks, city streets and short delivery windows. That makes them ideal for expedited freight, less-than-truckload (LTL) pieces, final-mile deliveries and time-sensitive parts.

Many box trucks fall under the federal weight threshold that requires a commercial driver's license, but rules vary by state and by what you haul, so check your own state and FMCSA requirements. Carriers running for hire across state lines also need the right authority and a USDOT number.

What box truck dispatch covers

  • Expedited and dedicated runs that pay for speed and availability.
  • Multi-stop routes with clear appointment windows.
  • Accessorials such as liftgate, inside delivery and residential delivery, priced into the rate.
  • Cargo insurance requirements that some brokers and shippers ask for.
  • Lane clustering, building a tight regional pattern so you spend less time deadheading.

Keeping box truck revenue steady

Box truck freight is shorter and more varied, so downtime between loads hurts more. A dispatcher who is reachable at any hour can grab time-critical loads that get posted in the evening or on weekends and keep your truck moving.
