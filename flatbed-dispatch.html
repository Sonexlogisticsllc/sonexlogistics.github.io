Open-deck freight takes more skill

Flatbeds haul what will not fit in a box: steel coils and pipe, lumber, building materials, machinery and equipment. Loading is usually from the side or top, and securing the load is part of the job.

Flatbed rates can be strong because fewer trucks can do the work properly. You pay for that in time, with tarping, strapping and waiting, so the right question is what a load pays per hour, not just per mile.

What flatbed dispatch covers

  • Tarp requirements and fees written on the rate confirmation before you accept.
  • Dimensions and permits. Loads over legal width, height or weight need permits, which the carrier obtains for each state on the route. Your dispatcher flags dimensions before booking.
  • Securement equipment. Confirm the straps, chains, binders, corner protectors and tarps the load needs.
  • Appointment and loading windows, since many shippers load first-come, first-served.
  • Weather and seasonal swings, which move flatbed demand and rates through the year.

Keeping flatbed weeks profitable

Reloads on a flatbed depend heavily on where you deliver. Industrial and construction regions tend to have more outbound flatbed freight than rural delivery points. Planning the next load before you deliver the current one is how drivers avoid long deadheads.
