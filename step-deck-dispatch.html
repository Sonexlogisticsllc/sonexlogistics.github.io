What a step deck does

A step deck, also called a drop deck, has an upper deck over the tractor and a lower main deck behind it. The lower deck sits closer to the road, so taller freight stays under the legal height limit. In most states that limit is 13 feet 6 inches, and a step deck lets cargo stand roughly 10 feet tall.

Typical loads include construction equipment, tall machinery, large vessels and anything that is too tall for a standard flatbed but does not need a specialty trailer.

Over-dimensional loads: where mistakes get expensive

Once freight exceeds legal width, height, length or weight, it is over-dimensional. That brings permits, route restrictions and sometimes escorts. Some states limit when oversize loads may travel, for example daylight only, or no weekend or holiday movement.

  • Check dimensions before you say yes. A few inches can change the permit class.
  • Confirm who pays for permits and escorts. Usually the carrier buys them, so the cost belongs in the negotiated rate.
  • Plan the route and the clock. Travel restrictions affect delivery dates and your next load.

What step deck dispatch adds

Specialized freight pays better when it is booked correctly and costs you money when it is not. Your dispatcher collects dimensions and weights on the first call, flags states and routes that complicate the move, and negotiates for permit and escort costs before you commit.
