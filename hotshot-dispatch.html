What hotshot hauling is

Hotshot carriers pull a gooseneck or bumper-pull flatbed trailer, typically 30 to 40 feet, behind a heavy-duty pickup. They are called when freight cannot wait for a full-size truck: replacement parts, equipment, building materials and small machinery.

Speed is the product. That means time, availability and quick answers matter more than almost anything else on the load.

What to get right before you book

  • Weight ratings. Your truck and trailer ratings decide how much you can legally haul.
  • CDL requirements. Whether you need a CDL depends on the combined weight rating of your truck and trailer. Many setups stay under the threshold and heavier ones do not, so check your numbers and the rules.
  • Authority and insurance. Hauling for hire across state lines needs the proper authority, a USDOT number and insurance that meets the broker's requirements.
  • Securement. Hotshot loads are open-deck, so straps, chains and tarps need to match the freight.

Why a 24/7 dispatcher matters for hotshots

Urgent loads are posted at all hours and are gone quickly. A dispatcher who monitors loads around the clock, negotiates the rate and handles the paperwork lets you accept urgent freight while you are already driving. You keep the final decision on every load.
